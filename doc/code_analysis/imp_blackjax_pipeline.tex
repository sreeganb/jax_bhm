\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note documents the string-of-beads IMP\,/\,BlackJAX pipeline in enough
detail that the next person to touch it --- most likely me, six months from now
--- does not have to re-derive its structure from the source. The emphasis is
deliberately not on what each function returns. It is on \emph{where each
representation of the system lives}, \emph{which of the two available scoring
paths is actually being evaluated}, \emph{where compilation happens and how
often}, and \emph{which degrees of freedom are genuinely being sampled as
opposed to merely being present in the model}. The last of these has already
cost me more debugging time than everything else combined, and it is treated at
length.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and the short version}

The entrypoint under discussion is
\code{imp\_testing/string\_of\_beads/run\_imp\_flexible\_blackjax.py},
together with the two modules it delegates to,
\code{sampling/imp\_blackjax\_adapter.py} and
\code{sampling/wrapper\_imp\_blackjax.py}.

The architecture can be summarised in five statements, each of which is
expanded on below.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item IMP owns the physics. The topology, hierarchy, restraints, and the
    definition of what counts as a rigid body are all constructed on the IMP
    side and are never re-derived elsewhere.
  \item An adapter layer converts that model into a flat vector of real numbers
    together with a pure JAX function mapping that vector to a scalar energy.
  \item BlackJAX never sees an IMP hierarchy. It is handed a flat vector and a
    log-density callable, and it returns flat vectors. It is a sampler, not a
    model.
  \item The hot path is the JAX score evaluation, not the Python control loop
    and not proposal generation.
  \item Which variables are sampled is decided by a single configuration
    string. Rigid bodies exist in the model and contribute to the energy
    regardless, but they are only \emph{moved} when that string selects them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Three representations of the same system}

Most of the confusion in this codebase comes from the fact that the same
physical assembly is represented three times, in three incompatible formats,
and that the conversions between them are where the bugs live.

\paragraph{The IMP hierarchy.}
This is the authoritative representation. It is built in
\code{build\_imp\_system()} by \code{IMP.pmi.topology.TopologyReader}, which
parses \code{data/topology.txt}, followed by
\code{IMP.pmi.macros.BuildSystem}, which materialises the hierarchy and the
degrees-of-freedom bookkeeping. Connectivity restraints are added per molecule
via \code{IMP.pmi.restraints.stereochemistry.ConnectivityRestraint}, and
explicit harmonic upper-bound restraints between matched residues of the two
coils are added as raw \code{IMP.core.DistanceRestraint} objects. The result is
a graph of states, molecules, fragments, and particles, decorated with
\code{XYZR}, \code{RigidBody}, and \code{RigidMember} as appropriate, plus a
list of restraints that is finally wrapped into an
\code{IMP.core.RestraintsScoringFunction}.

For the test system this yields four rigid bodies (residues 1--21 and 32--52 of
each of KCOIL and ECOIL) and twenty flexible beads (residues 22--31 of each),
for 104 hierarchy leaves out of 1904 model particles.

\paragraph{The JAX model dictionary.}
Calling \code{sf\_imp.\_get\_jax()} returns an interface object exposing
\code{get\_jax\_model()} and \code{score\_func}. The model dictionary contains
two arrays, \code{xyz} and \code{r}, which are exports of IMP's internal sphere
table.

This detail is worth stating precisely, because a great deal of unnecessary
machinery was written before it was understood. \textbf{The sphere table is
indexed by particle index.} Row $i$ of \code{jm['xyz']} corresponds to
\code{ParticleIndex(i)}, for every $i$, with no exceptions and no permutation.
Particles that carry no \code{XYZR} decorator --- hierarchy nodes, residues,
chains, and so forth --- occupy their row as $(+\infty,+\infty,+\infty)$ with
radius $+\infty$. In the test system 1560 of the 1904 rows are finite and 344
are placeholders. Any code that reconstructs this mapping by matching
coordinates is solving a problem that does not exist, and is moreover fragile:
the placeholder rows participate in the nearest-neighbour search and two beads
at coincident positions will be assigned arbitrarily.

\paragraph{The flat DOF vector.}
BlackJAX samples $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and nothing else. The mapping
between $\theta$ and the sphere table is established by
\code{IMPDOFSpace.from\_imp(...)} and consumed by \code{IMPSMCAdapter}. The
layout is
\[
  \theta = \bigl[\underbrace{t_1, q_1, \ldots, t_{K}, q_{K}}_{\text{7 per rigid body}},\;
                 \underbrace{x_1, y_1, z_1, \ldots}_{\text{3 per flexible bead}}\bigr],
\]
where $t_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is a rigid-body translation and $q_k \in
\mathbb{R}^4$ is a quaternion stored scalar-first as $[w,x,y,z]$.

Every sphere-table row that is not written by this decoding is frozen at
whatever value it held when the DOF space was constructed. That is the
mechanism by which a mode selection silences part of the system, and it is
silent by design.

\section{Rigid bodies: what IMP hands over, and what must be handed back}

\subsection{The extraction side}

\code{IMP.core.\_jax\_rigid.\_get\_rigid\_bodies(model)} returns everything
needed to parameterise the rigid degrees of freedom, and it should be used in
preference to reconstructing any of it:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \code{intcoord}, a particle-indexed array of \emph{body-frame}
    coordinates;
  \item \code{quaternion}, one $[w,x,y,z]$ per rigid body, indexed by
    \code{rb\_index};
  \item \code{rb\_index\_from\_particle}, mapping the rigid-body \emph{particle}
    index to \code{rb\_index};
  \item \code{bodies}, a list carrying \code{particle\_index},
    \code{member\_particle\_indexes}, and \code{body\_member\_indexes} for
    nested bodies;
  \item \code{non\_rigid\_members}, empty in the present system.
\end{itemize}

The relation that ties these together, and which has been verified numerically
against IMP's own expansion to within the precision of the printed output, is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:expand}
  \mathrm{xyz}[p] \;=\; R\bigl(q_{\,b(p)}\bigr)\,\mathrm{intcoord}[p]
                    \;+\; \mathrm{xyz}\bigl[\pi(b(p))\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $b(p)$ is the rigid body containing member $p$, $\pi(b)$ is that body's
own particle index, and $R(\cdot)$ is the standard rotation matrix of a unit
quaternion. Two consequences follow.

First, the rigid-body translation is not stored separately: it \emph{is} the
sphere-table row of the rigid-body particle. In the test system rows
$1900$--$1903$ hold the four reference-frame origins. Any decoder that rewrites
member rows but leaves those four rows stale produces an internally
inconsistent model.

Second, \code{intcoord} is the body-frame coordinate, not a world-frame offset
from the centre. Computing $\mathrm{xyz}[p] - \mathrm{com}$ instead and then
applying $R(q)$ to it applies the initial rotation twice, so that
$\mathrm{decode}(\mathrm{encode}(\cdot))$ is not the identity. The
parameterisation remains a valid chart on $SE(3)$ --- the reachable set is
unchanged --- but the encoded state no longer corresponds to the actual IMP
configuration, which quietly invalidates warm starts and any comparison between
the JAX and C++ scores.

\subsection{The write-back side, and why trajectories appear frozen}

Sampling produces flat vectors. Writing them back into IMP is required for RMF
output and for any subsequent IMP-side analysis, and it is the step where the
most time has been lost.

\textbf{Rigid-body members must be written through the reference frame.} The
global coordinate of a \code{RigidMember} is a derived quantity. IMP maintains a
\code{ScoreState} that recomputes it from the body's reference frame on the next
\code{model.update()}, and both \code{RMF} frame saving and
\code{ScoringFunction.evaluate()} trigger that update. Consequently the
apparently obvious
\begin{center}
\code{IMP.core.XYZ(member).set\_coordinates(...)}
\end{center}
is a no-op for anything belonging to a rigid body: the assignment is made and
then immediately reverted. The correct operation is
\begin{center}
\code{IMP.core.RigidBody(model, pi).set\_reference\_frame(} \\
\code{IMP.algebra.ReferenceFrame3D(} \\
\code{IMP.algebra.Transformation3D(Rotation3D(w,x,y,z), Vector3D(t))))}
\end{center}
followed by \code{model.update()}. Flexible beads are free particles and may be
written directly.

The failure mode this produces is maximally confusing: the sampler runs, the
score changes, the acceptance rate looks reasonable, and the trajectory is
static. Because the same writer is used for flexible beads, where it works
correctly, the symptom appears only when rigid-body sampling is switched on,
which naturally directs suspicion at the rigid-body code rather than at the
output path.

\subsection{Non-rigid members}

The present topology declares none, and the extraction path asserts as much.
This is a placeholder rather than a limitation of principle. When a non-rigid
selection is added, those particles acquire three sampled degrees of freedom
each \emph{in the body frame}, and appear in \code{non\_rigid\_members} rather
than as free beads. Treating them as flexible beads and writing world
coordinates would be wrong in a way that no current test would catch, so the
assertion should stay until the case is handled explicitly.

\section{The two scoring paths}

The pipeline contains two distinct implementations of the energy, and it is
essential to know which one is being evaluated.

\paragraph{The C++ path.}
\code{IMPLogPosterior} in \code{wrapper\_imp\_blackjax.py} unpacks the flat
vector into the IMP model, calls \code{sf\_imp.evaluate(False)}, and returns the
scalar. This routes through IMP's C++ scoring machinery. It is exact and it is
the reference, but it is impure --- it mutates global IMP state --- and it can
be neither JIT-compiled nor vectorised. Every evaluation is a Python-to-C++
round trip.

\paragraph{The JAX path.}
\code{IMPSMCAdapter} decodes the flat vector to a full \code{xyz} array with
\code{\_flat\_to\_xyz}, substitutes it into a frozen copy of the base model
dictionary, and calls \code{ji.score\_func(jm)}. This is pure, compilable, and
vectorisable over particles, which is what makes SMC tractable.

Agreement between the two has been confirmed directly: for the unshuffled test
system both report $64727.965$. Note, though, that the JAX value is
$64727.965$ against the C++ value $64727.96513564067$. That is single precision,
and at an energy of $6.5\times 10^4$ the absolute resolution of
\code{float32} is roughly $4\times10^{-3}$ --- larger than many of the score
differences a Metropolis ratio is required to resolve. Enabling
\code{jax\_enable\_x64} before the first JAX array is created is therefore not
an optimisation but a correctness requirement.

The log-likelihood exposed to BlackJAX is
\begin{equation}
  \log L(\theta) \;=\; -\,\frac{E(\theta)}{k_{\mathrm{B}}T},
\end{equation}
with $E$ the IMP energy. The sign convention matters: IMP minimises, BlackJAX
maximises.

\paragraph{A methodological caution.}
Because RMH was originally wired to the C++ path and SMC to the JAX path, any
comparison between them confounded the sampler with the score implementation
and, in the original wiring, with the choice of degrees of freedom as well. For
a defensible comparison all samplers must consume the same
\code{adapter.log\_prob} over the same DOF space, differing only in the sampling
algorithm. The C++ path is best retained as a test oracle rather than as a
production route.

\section{Compilation: where it happens, and how often}

\paragraph{Adapter level.}
\code{IMPSMCAdapter.\_build\_jax\_fns()} compiles five functions once at
construction: \code{\_flat\_to\_xyz}, \code{\_score}, \code{log\_likelihood},
\code{log\_prior}, and \code{log\_prob}. This is the intended behaviour and the
cost is amortised immediately.

It is worth noting that constructing an adapter is therefore not free. A
parameterised test suite that builds one adapter per test compiles dozens of
executables whose device binaries accumulate; on a GPU this eventually surfaces
as \code{RESOURCE\_EXHAUSTED: Failed to load in-memory CUBIN (compiled for a
different GPU?)}, which is CUDA's generic wording for an out-of-memory
condition while loading a module and has nothing to do with architecture
mismatch. Small diagnostic work belongs on the CPU backend.

\paragraph{Sampler level.}
In \code{smc\_base\_sampler.py} the mutation function and the weight function
are decorated with \code{jax.jit} \emph{inside} the loop over temperature steps.
Each iteration closes over a fresh tempered log-density carrying a new
$\lambda$, so JAX sees a new function object and recompiles. With a hundred
temperature steps and fifty MCMC sweeps per step, that is a hundred separate
compilations of a scan over fifty kernel applications. Lifting these out of the
loop and passing $\lambda$ and $\Delta\lambda$ as traced arguments is the single
largest available speed-up.

\paragraph{Implication.}
The Python loop is not compiled and need not be. It orchestrates the algorithm;
the per-particle work runs inside compiled code. Profiling attention belongs on
the score evaluation and on compilation count, not on loop overhead.

\section{What BlackJAX actually does}

BlackJAX supplies sampler mechanics and no physics.

\paragraph{Random-walk Metropolis--Hastings.}
Given an initial position, a log-density, and a proposal rule, each step
generates a proposal, evaluates the log-density there, forms the Metropolis
ratio, and returns either the proposed or the current state. Because JAX arrays
are immutable, the returned state is a new value rather than a mutation; the
outer loop stores it. Running the whole chain under \code{jax.lax.scan} compiles
it once instead of once per step, and removes the Python round trip entirely
provided the log-density is pure.

\paragraph{Sequential Monte Carlo.}
At each tempering step the particle weights are updated by the incremental
log-weight
\begin{equation}
  \Delta w_t(\theta) \;=\; (\lambda_t - \lambda_{t-1})\,\log L(\theta),
\end{equation}
particles are resampled according to the normalised weights, and each survivor
is mutated by repeated applications of an MCMC kernel targeting
$\pi_t \propto \pi_0 \, L^{\lambda_t}$. The fixed-schedule implementation
computes the increments explicitly and calls \code{blackjax.smc.base.step};
the adaptive variant lets BlackJAX choose $\lambda_t$ to hit a target effective
sample size.

\paragraph{A scaling caveat.}
The validity of this construction assumes the initial population is distributed
according to the prior. It presently is not: \code{sample\_prior} draws a
Gaussian cloud around the current configuration while \code{log\_prior} is a
flat box. More seriously, the energy scale matters enormously. After shuffling,
the test system reports an energy of $1.5\times10^6$; with $k_{\mathrm{B}}T=1$
the first geometric step ($\Delta\lambda \approx 10^{-4}$) spreads the
log-weights across particles by two to three orders of magnitude, and systematic
resampling collapses the population onto a single ancestor immediately. The
best-particle-per-step trajectory then looks nearly static \emph{even when the
degrees of freedom and the write-back are entirely correct}. Distinguishing this
from a plumbing failure requires monitoring the effective sample size and the
spread of the particle cloud, not just the trajectory.

\section{Proposals}

The RMH driver prefers an adapter-supplied proposal when one exists, falling
back to an isotropic Gaussian of width \code{rmh\_sigma} otherwise. The
adapter's \code{make\_rmh\_proposal\_fn} exposes three independent scales:
$\sigma_{\mathrm{rb,trans}}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{rb,rot}}$, and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{fb}}$.

Two points deserve emphasis.

\paragraph{The generic \code{rmh\_sigma} may be inert.}
When a block-structured proposal is supplied it takes precedence, and the
effective step sizes come from \code{suggested\_rmh\_proposal()}. Passing
\code{rmh\_sigma=2.0} and then reasoning about a two-\AA{} step is a reliable
route to confusion.

\paragraph{The rotation proposal must be multiplicative.}
Orientations are updated by left-multiplication with a small rotation,
$q' = \delta q \otimes q$ with $\delta q = \exp(\mathbf{v}/2)$ and $\mathbf{v}$
Gaussian. This proposal is symmetric with respect to the Haar measure on
$SO(3)$, so the plain Metropolis ratio is correct and no Jacobian correction is
required. The fallback isotropic Gaussian on the raw four-vector is a
\emph{different} measure. Since the decoder normalises the quaternion, the
target is invariant to its norm, so the norm performs an unconstrained random
walk; as it grows, a fixed-width additive step corresponds to an ever-smaller
rotation, and the bodies gradually stop turning. Silently falling back to that
proposal is therefore worse than failing loudly, and the exception handler that
used to do so has been removed.

A related point concerns initialisation. Drawing orientations as the encoded
quaternion perturbed by a jitter of $0.1$ radians leaves every particle at
essentially the same orientation, which reproduces the appearance of frozen
rigid bodies from a completely different cause. A genuinely uniform draw ---
a normalised isotropic four-dimensional Gaussian, which is uniform on $S^3$ and
hence on $SO(3)$ up to the $\pm q$ double cover --- is what the tempered-SMC
prior claims to be and is the correct default.

\section{Which variables are sampled}

This is controlled by the mode string passed to \code{IMPDOFSpace.from\_imp}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Mode & Rigid bodies & Flexible beads \\
\midrule
\code{flex}  & frozen at template & sampled \\
\code{rigid} & sampled            & frozen at template \\
\code{all}   & sampled            & sampled \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Under \code{flex} the rigid-body descriptors are not even constructed:
\code{rb\_descriptors} is empty and $n$ counts only bead coordinates. The bodies
still contribute to the energy, since their frozen coordinates are carried
through from the base model, but no sampler can move them. This is correct
behaviour and it is also, on its own, a complete explanation for a run in which
nothing rotates. It should be the first thing checked, and the DOF summary
printed at adapter construction should state the rigid-body count explicitly so
that it cannot be overlooked.

\section{Output}

RMF frames are written in two places: during RMH via a step callback, and after
SMC by decoding the best particle from each temperature step. In both cases the
flat state must be pushed through the reference-frame write-back described in
Section~3.2 before the frame is saved. Decoding to \code{xyz} and assigning leaf
coordinates directly is an export path that works for beads and silently fails
for rigid bodies.

\section{Invariants worth testing}

The following are cheap, local, and each fails for exactly one reason, which is
what makes them useful.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item $\log p(\theta) = \log \pi_0(\theta) + \log L(\theta)$ identically.
  \item $\mathrm{decode}(\mathrm{encode}())$ reproduces the sphere table exactly
    on all finite rows. This catches any body-frame inconsistency.
  \item The JAX and C++ scores agree to double precision at the encoded state.
  \item All pairwise distances within each rigid body are invariant under an
    arbitrary $(t,q)$. This is the defining property of the parameterisation.
  \item Perturbing a rigid body's degrees of freedom displaces its members by a
    non-negligible amount. Guards directly against the frozen-trajectory
    symptom.
  \item Writing a moved state into IMP and calling \code{model.update()}
    reproduces the decoded geometry. This is the decisive round trip.
  \item Assigning member \code{XYZ} directly is reverted by the
    \code{ScoreState}. Worth asserting explicitly so the diagnosis in
    Section~3.2 is recorded in executable form rather than in a comment.
  \item In each mode, exactly the intended sphere-table rows change and the
    remainder are frozen.
  \item A block-structured proposal perturbs only its own block, and quaternion
    norms remain unity.
  \item The prior spreads orientations rather than concentrating on the starting
    pose.
  \item The effective sample size does not collapse over a short SMC run.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}

The pipeline joins three layers. IMP defines the physics and owns the
authoritative representation. The adapter converts that representation into a
flat latent vector and a pure, compilable score, and --- equally importantly ---
converts sampled vectors back into IMP through the reference frame. BlackJAX
performs RMH or SMC on the latent vector and knows nothing about proteins.

Nearly all of the difficulty encountered so far has lived at the two boundaries
rather than inside any of the three layers: the particle-index mapping on the
way in, and the rigid-body write-back on the way out. Both are now pinned by
explicit invariants, and both should stay pinned.
